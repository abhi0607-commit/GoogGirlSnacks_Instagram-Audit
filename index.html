01 · Audit finding

The bio led with retailers

The original bio listed retailers first and promoted the new Grab & Go format. Those were useful buying cues, but a first-time visitor had to read through the list to understand what Good Girl Snacks sells and why they might want to try it.

  • Search and recognition.The original display name matched the handle. Adding Hot Girl Pickles introduces the product wording shoppers see on the packaging.
  • Product value.Crunch and the two formats give shoppers a reason to pay attention.
  • Shopping action.A direct locator link gives the shopper a clearer next step than a generic domain.

Scope note. Working audience assumption: U.S. shoppers looking for a crunchy, savory snack. The audit used the supplied profile screenshot and the brand’s public shop and store locator. Posting cadence, pin status, and account performance were not verified.

06 · Rollout and measurement

What I’d test first

PriorityActionImpact and effort
1Update the name and four-line bio. Check the result on mobile.High impactLow effort
2Put the locator first and add link tracking. Test a ZIP search on mobile.High impactLow effort
3Pin the product introduction, flavor guide, and retail post if suitable assets exist.High impactLow effort with existing posts
4Refresh the Highlight covers and opening Stories.Medium impactLow effort
5Run a four-week recipe Reel test and consider a creator taste test.High impactMedium effort

Proposed measurement plan

Discovery

Non-follower reach and follows

Tells me whether the new name and bio help people who don’t follow the account yet find it and stay.

Recipe usefulness

Saves and shares

Tells me whether recipe Reels are worth keeping, because a save means someone plans to come back.

Shopping intent

Instagram-referred locator visits

Tells me whether the bio link and retail posts move people toward a store search.

These are proposed measures for future testing. No performance results are claimed.
